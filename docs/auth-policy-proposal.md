# Authentication, authorization and guest policy

Status: T007 design proposal. This document distinguishes accepted policy and limited owner-approved design decisions from unresolved proposals. It does not grant G-SECURITY approval, authorize implementation, or change roles or privileges. Constitution baseline 2026-10-29, SHA-256 `426923873e828b7a0e8fd63b88a5a9b5e01b35f9c2e3bb965c322931d1d9835a`. The constitution prevails. Sources: [project plan, sections 10 and 19](../project_plan.md), [security decision proposals](security-decisions-proposal.md), [remaining security decisions](security-open-decisions.md), and ADRs [0003](adr/0003-lost-mfa-recovery.md), [0004](adr/0004-security-decisions.md), [0005](adr/0005-user-password-recovery.md), [0006](adr/0006-security-directions.md) and [0007](adr/0007-csrf-contract.md).

## Accepted policy and limited approved decisions

The following items restate accepted plan requirements or the explicitly limited decisions in ADRs 0003–0007. They are not approval of implementation details beyond those scopes.

### Identity, roles and authorization

- The access service determines identity and effective role from authenticated, revocable server-side sessions. Deny by default and enforce authorization server-side at every request, tool and stored-object boundary; never trust a client/model-supplied role or hidden UI control.
- The [project plan's section 10.3 capability matrix](../project_plan.md) remains authoritative; this proposal adds no capabilities. Users receive only its listed user capabilities, scoped to their own data and actions. Admins receive only its listed admin capabilities and perform administrative actions through the web app. A linked channel has at most `user` privilege, including when linked by an admin. Guests may ask general questions subject to fixed directives, using only the local model and temporary, session-scoped context; they have no voice, file/image upload, persistent/global memory, tools, sandbox, cron, skills, messaging, external model or display. No channel, confirmation or reauthentication flow may add a capability or elevate a role.
- Recheck authorization when work executes and after account, role, session or channel changes. Cancel or safely reconcile work whose authorization is revoked; do not blindly repeat side effects. Preserve users' ability to delete all their own memories even when an admin hides them.

### Passwords, login throttling and MFA

- Password verifiers use Argon2id. The accepted starting parameters are 19 MiB memory, two iterations and parallelism one, with a unique random salt and encoded parameters; rehash after a successful login when approved parameters change. Measure latency and contention on the target Pi before hardware acceptance.
- Require at least 15 Unicode code points and allow at least 64; accept Unicode and spaces, normalize to NFC consistently before validation and hashing, do not impose composition rules or periodic rotation, and never truncate. Reject common or compromised whole-password values. The owner selected manual/offline checking only: an authorized operator supplies and manually updates a local list; do not send passwords or derived identifiers to a remote service. Fail closed for any new or changed password, including first-admin bootstrap, if the required local list is absent or unusable; existing login remains available. On 2026-10-31 the owner authorized read-only public research of offline sources and then selected [HIBP Pwned Passwords](https://haveibeenpwned.com/Passwords) as the candidate for a detailed offline acquisition/import proposal only. Its official page describes the corpus as freely available and links an offline downloader; [HIBP Terms of Use](https://haveibeenpwned.com/TermsOfUse) §1.3 permits lawful use of Pwned Passwords and downloadable hash sets, including commercial use, and says attribution is not required for Pwned Passwords. The terms also contain restrictions on distributing HIBP data and operating a substantially similar service; their applicability to any contemplated handling requires legal review, and no redistribution is intended. The downloader is separately [BSD 3-Clause licensed](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HaveIBeenPwned/PwnedPasswordsDownloader/main/LICENSE) and its README requires .NET 10 or later; it is not selected or approved. No password corpus was downloaded or imported. The owner selected the source integrity model and 90-day maximum age; exact snapshot/version, import implementation, legal applicability and approval of HIBP as the production source remain open.
- **Offline list requirements (owner-selected 2026-10-31; implementation not approved):** Manual acquisition only from the official HIBP source; keep network/API password checks disabled. Accept HTTPS to the official source plus validation of its per-range `Content-MD5` response headers for transfer integrity and compute a local SHA-256 manifest for snapshot identity. The owner accepts that this does not provide an independent publisher signature; the MD5 header and ETags are supplied by the same service. The official downloader README says it checks `Content-MD5` per range and uses ETags for incremental refresh, but the downloader itself is not approved. Require the complete expected range set; record source URL, snapshot/version where available, retrieval time, record count, size and SHA-256; stage and validate the whole snapshot before atomic activation, retaining the previous list on any failure. A list older than 90 days, absent, corrupt, or failing the agreed verification is unusable; fail closed for password creation/change and initial bootstrap, while existing login remains available. Never log candidate passwords or derived hashes; keep Argon2id account verifiers separate from local SHA-1 blocklist lookup. Exact manual tooling/import format, source-specific legal review, and production-source approval remain open.
- Use generic authentication failures. The accepted account-delay starting point is a 15-minute delay after five failures for an account within 15 minutes, without permanent lockout. The owner selected that the fixed 15-minute delay begins on the fifth failure and is not extended by further attempts during the delay. Block all login attempts for that account until expiry, including attempts with a correct password; reject them before password verification and do not create a session. Attempts rejected during the active delay do not produce individual failed-password audit events or alter the counter. Proposed public behavior: use the same generic authentication status/body and no account-specific headers for blocked, wrong-password and unknown-account outcomes; do not add `Retry-After`. Because pre-verification rejection can produce a timing difference, include timing-distribution tests and document any residual signal for G-SECURITY review; do not add artificial delay without separate review. A successful login outside an active delay resets that account's failure counter to zero; propose coordinating this reset with session creation and its required lifecycle audit event in one SQLite transaction when they share the database. The owner selected a service-wide admission limit before password verification, using a continuously refilled token bucket with bounded burst; numeric rate/burst remain open; after restart, initialize the bucket empty and refill continuously, accepting temporary login unavailability: when exhausted, reject generically without verification or session creation, and without incrementing account failure state. This preserves the per-attempt audit rule for actual failed verifications. The owner selected HTTP 503/`UNAVAILABLE`, a generic error body and no `Retry-After`; numeric limits, reset behavior and exact body schema remain open for synthetic-volume and G-SECURITY/G-CONTRACT review; do not depend on untrusted client IP/Origin. Apply the per-account counter only when the attempted account resolves to an existing principal; do not persist a per-account counter for unknown account names. Trusted-origin-based limits remain open pending the proxy/origin boundary.
- The owner selected a minimal security-audit event for every failed password verification attempt, retained for 90 days subject to operational and applicable legal review, distinct from attempts rejected before verification during an active delay or service-wide admission control and from the aggregate-signal-only policy for failed `auth.resolve_session` cookie checks. Never include the submitted password, submitted account-name text, verifier or other credential material. Include `principal_id` only when the attempted account exists; for an unknown account, include only access-service identity and no account correlation value. Credential failures remain generic. A failed password verification for an existing account counts toward that account's five-failure delay even if its audit write fails and the request returns `UNAVAILABLE`/503; unknown-account attempts do not create per-account counters. If the required event cannot be persisted, deny login and return `UNAVAILABLE`/503 without credential or account-existence details. The owner selected evaluating a same-SQLite direction in which the counter is committed in a separate transaction before the audit append is attempted; if the append then fails, the counter remains committed. The owner scoped this guarantee to audit-append failure while SQLite remains available. A failed counter commit or unavailable shared database returns `UNAVAILABLE`/503 without a guarantee that the attempt was counted. For a successful login outside a delay, propose one transaction for session creation, counter reset and required lifecycle-event append when those records share one SQLite database; if the event cannot be committed, abort all three. These are proposal directions only. Counter durability, failure semantics and recovery remain subject to T029/G-SECURITY/G-CONTRACT review.
- **Owner-selected login-flow direction (2026-11-01; schema not approved):** accept a username identifier only (not an email identifier) and submit password plus an optional TOTP code in one login request, rather than a separate second-factor challenge. Usernames are ASCII-only and normalized to lowercase ASCII for lookup/uniqueness; uppercase ASCII input maps to the same canonical username. The canonical username must match `[a-z0-9._-]{3,32}`. Do not silently trim or apply additional punctuation/reserved-name rules. The internal `auth.login` request uses an empty `{}` context, with service identity derived from the authenticated peer; it does not reuse the `pre_auth` variant reserved for `auth.resolve_session`. The access service alone validates browser Origin, the pre-auth cookie and CSRF token; Python trusts the authenticated access-service identity for that validation and receives no browser token or caller assertion in context. The accepted CSRF contract still requires atomic pre-session invalidation and fresh session creation; the mechanism satisfying this across service layers and crash recovery remains unresolved and must not be represented as implemented or verified. A successful login returns HTTP 204 with no body, sets the authenticated session cookie and clears the pre-auth cookie; the client must fetch a fresh CSRF token before its next mutation. For an account without TOTP, reject a supplied `totp_code` with the same generic authentication failure as other login failures; do not reveal enrollment state. Exact invalid-input response, cookie-header details and audit handling for TOTP-only failures remain open for G-CONTRACT/G-SECURITY review. Password normalization and generic failure policy above still apply.
- **Owner-selected TOTP login-failure direction (2026-11-01; implementation not approved):** after the password has verified for an existing account, a missing, invalid or replayed code for an enrolled account, or an unexpected code for an account without TOTP, counts as one failure in the same five-failures/15-minute account counter and emits a security-audit event. The existing fixed 15-minute delay begins on the fifth combined password/TOTP failure and is not extended. A rejected login increments the counter only once. Keep the public failure generic and create no session. For these TOTP/unexpected-field failures, commit the counter increment and audit event atomically; if audit persistence fails, roll both back and return `UNAVAILABLE`/503. This differs from the separately selected password-verification-failure sequence, which commits the counter before attempting audit. While TOTP audit storage is unavailable, no counter-increment guarantee is made; G-SECURITY/T029 must review this availability tradeoff. Event schema, retention and remaining recovery details are still open for G-CONTRACT/G-SECURITY review.
- TOTP is optional for `admin` and `user`; passkeys are deferred until after beta. The approved TOTP starting proposal is a unique random secret, 30-second step, six digits, current plus one adjacent step on either side, with rate limiting and atomic rejection of an already accepted step. Enroll only after recent reauthentication and confirmation with a valid code. Never log or expose seeds to the agent or downloadable backups. The owner selected ten single-use recovery codes per account, each with 128 bits of randomness, shown once and stored only as verifiers; each consumed code is invalidated. An application admin may reset an ordinary user's TOTP after recent reauthentication, explicit confirmation and mandatory audit; that user must re-enroll. Recovery codes do not replace the authorized OS-admin procedure for an admin who has lost all factors.

### Account sessions, reset and recovery

- Generate the cookie bearer token from 32 cryptographically random bytes and encode it as canonical unpadded base64url of exactly 43 ASCII characters. After canonical decoding, store only SHA-256 of the 32 token bytes as its server-side verifier; reject padding, noncanonical encodings and values that do not decode to exactly 32 bytes. Keep the token secret and distinct from the UUIDv4 `session_id`; never store the raw token or log, cache or return it. Deliver it in the owner-selected `__Host-superharry_session` cookie with `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict`, `Path=/` and no `Domain`, over HTTPS. Per the owner's 2026-10-31 selection, apply a 60-minute idle limit and 24-hour absolute account-session limit; background polling or SSE alone does not extend inactivity. Rotate after authentication and privilege changes; revoke on logout, password reset, account disablement and role changes. Reject expired/revoked sessions and prevent fixation. Guest expiry remains separately 24 hours. Exact storage/schema, rate-limit counters and persistence, and deployment remain gated. If a required session lifecycle audit event cannot be durably committed, abort the associated transition and return `UNAVAILABLE`/503; a failed logout leaves the session unrevoked.
- Owner-selected quota behavior: after valid credentials are verified, when an account's active-session quota is reached, reject creation of a new session and preserve all existing sessions rather than silently evicting one. Do not count this as a failed credential attempt. Return the same external response as a failed login, with no quota-specific details, and emit an individual security-audit event for each rejection. Limit event data to authenticated `principal_id`, fixed event type/outcome, timestamp and `request_id`; exclude IP, Origin, credentials, token and quota/cap details. Retain these events for 90 days, subject to legal/operational review. If the event cannot be persisted, return `UNAVAILABLE`/503 rather than the normal generic quota response. The numeric cap remains open pending capacity evidence; event schema and failure/transaction behavior remain subject to T029 and G-SECURITY/G-CONTRACT review.
- The owner selected not auditing every `UNAUTHENTICATED` `auth.resolve_session` failure; use five failures associated with an identifiable account in a rolling 15-minute window as a security-signal threshold only, not a new delay rule. Emit at most one signal per account per 15-minute interval with that account's `principal_id` and an aggregate count, rather than one event per threshold block; after a signal, another may be emitted no sooner than 15 minutes later if the rolling window still meets the threshold. Do not infer an account from an unidentifiable cookie or add origin-based signals before GSEC-04 approves the trusted-origin boundary. Retain signal events for 90 days, consistent with security-attempt events and separate from diagnostic logs. Keep the response cause-neutral and never log the cookie token or cause-specific details. If a qualifying signal cannot be persisted, return `UNAVAILABLE`/503, not `UNAUTHENTICATED`/401, and do not issue context or update idle activity. The owner selected fail-closed behavior if ledger saturation prevents maintaining the active rolling count or persisting a qualifying signal: do not discard active timestamps; return `UNAVAILABLE`/503 without context or idle-activity update. Numeric storage bounds and capacity evidence remain open for synthetic-volume/ARM64 review. Exact counter persistence/reset and emission-boundary semantics, remaining event schema, cleanup/recovery, the selected 90-day duration and audit-failure behavior remain gated under T029/G-SECURITY and applicable legal review; separately required retry, nonce-replay and payload-conflict events still apply. Session lifecycle events for creation/login, explicit logout and revocation record the authenticated `principal_id` when available, or access-service identity for pre-authentication creation/login, plus canonical lowercase hyphenated UUIDv4 `session_id`, operation, outcome and timestamp; exclude IP address, cookie token and a separate revocation-reason field. Their selected 90-day retention remains subject to operational and applicable legal review.
- TOTP remains optional for both `admin` and `user`. Per the owner's 2026-10-31 selection, support both one-time recovery codes and administrator-assisted reset. This does not authorize an application administrator to bypass MFA for a lost admin factor: lost-admin recovery remains under ADR 0003's authorized OS-admin procedure with confirmation, audit and mandatory fresh MFA enrollment. Code generation, one-time verifier storage, presentation, reset authorization/reauthentication, audit and transaction details remain to be approved.
- A proposed reset token has 256 bits of randomness, is single-use, verifier-backed and expires after ten minutes. Submit it only through a protected body, never a URL or log; successful reset revokes applicable sessions and outstanding tokens. The owner selected ordinary-user password reset through an authorized OS administrator, retaining configured MFA and audit (ADR 0005). This does not select a token delivery or reset procedure, authorize email/messaging, or permit MFA bypass.
- For lost admin MFA, the owner accepted OS-admin recovery with explicit confirmation, audit and mandatory fresh MFA enrollment (ADR 0003). The approved design proposal constrains the resulting state to password-verified, short-lived MFA enrollment in the trusted web interface, with normal actions and channel use blocked until a new factor is verified. Recovery does not silently reopen first-run bootstrap.
- CSRF direction and the concrete browser contract in [ADR 0007](adr/0007-csrf-contract.md) are accepted within their stated scope. `GET /v1/session/csrf` issues the session's 32-byte random synchronizer token as unpadded base64url; it is session-bound, shared by tabs, not placed in a URL/cookie/log or browser persistent storage, and the response is not cached or exposed through CORS. Without a valid session, the endpoint may create only a bounded, privilege-free pre-authentication session with a host-only `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict`, `Path=/` cookie; its accepted lifetime is ten minutes absolute with no idle extension. Cookie-authenticated state-changing requests, including login and guest creation, send `X-CSRF-Token` and an exact configured HTTPS `Origin`; reject missing/null/ambiguous/malformed values before mutation and compare token bytes in constant time. Successful login or guest creation atomically replaces the pre-session and token with a fresh session and token; logout revokes and clears the session, and role changes rotate session and token. Tabs share the current token; stale tokens fail without a business side effect, and clients refresh only before a new intentional action, never automatically replaying a consequential request. `GET`, `HEAD` and `OPTIONS` have no application-side effects, except bounded pre-session allocation by the token endpoint; SSE does not extend account-session inactivity. This acceptance does not select pre-session quotas, cookie name, configured origins, proxy trust, schemas/storage, or MFA transition details.

### Guest admission and limits

The accepted plan permits a LAN/VPN “Continue as guest” entry without an account. A guest session expires after 24 hours and its temporary conversation context is deleted. Initial configurable limits are three guest sessions per hour per origin and ten questions per hour per session, with at most one queued or running question per session and a ten-minute queue timeout. Guest work is low priority and starts only when the device is otherwise idle; a guest response already in progress may finish before higher-priority work starts. Guest rates and role restrictions are enforced outside the model. Counter persistence and restart behavior, expiry cleanup and origin identity still need design approval.

### Proxy, confirmations and audit

- Access is LAN/VPN only; never expose the app publicly or port-forward it. Do not accept client-supplied forwarding or identity headers as proof of identity. Trust origin information only through an explicitly trusted proxy boundary; an IP address is a rate-limit signal, not a person identity. ADR 0006 selects one application-facing Caddy instance as a design direction, not a deployment configuration.
- Sensitive-action reauthentication has an approved starting proposal: password and, if configured, TOTP verified within five minutes. A one-use confirmation is bound to actor, session, action, target, exact parameters and current state, expires after five minutes, and is invalidated by session revocation or a relevant state change. Recheck authorization at execution. A confirmation records intent; it does not grant authority.
- Require confirmation for consequential browser actions, including purchases, sending messages, deletion and configuration changes, and follow the more restrictive applicable action and channel rules. Administrative changes remain web-only; channel requests cannot execute them.
- Keep privileged security audit records independent of optional diagnostics and without secrets or message content. Do not permit a privileged mutation when required audit persistence is unavailable. The exact durability and transaction behavior is unresolved.

## Proposed design refinements

The following are candidates for the remaining design work, not accepted policy. They must not be treated as implementation instructions until the relevant decisions and gates are approved.

1. **Origin derivation:** configure an explicit trusted immediate proxy; have it strip inbound forwarding headers and set a single authenticated client-origin value. Reject direct access to the internal listener, malformed or ambiguous values and untrusted chains. Normalize IPv4, IPv4-mapped IPv6 and IPv6 consistently; do not select an arbitrary `X-Forwarded-For` hop. The security-decisions proposal and security-open-decisions document describe this candidate; exact Caddy, OS boundary and Tailscale behavior need deployment verification.
2. **Abuse counters:** persist bounded account, origin and guest counters across restart; use generic errors and progressive temporary delays without attacker-triggered permanent lockout. Define rate windows, successful-login resets and retention only after evaluating shared NAT and address churn.
3. **Guest admission:** apply rate limits before creating a guest session and before enqueuing a question. Expiry must remove context and invalidate the session, including after restart; queue cancellation, shutdown and expired work must not retain context or permit duplicate side effects. Do not silently reset counters on restart.
4. **Administrative confirmation:** keep authentication, role authorization, recent reauthentication and action confirmation as distinct checks. Bind confirmation to the exact operation and re-evaluate current permissions/state immediately before execution. For work that may outlast the five-minute reauthentication window, do not assume the initial confirmation remains valid; require an approved continuation or cancellation rule.
5. **Session and CSRF handling:** preserve the accepted ADR 0007 contract, including bounded privilege-free pre-authentication state and no automatic replay after stale-token rejection. Decide its pre-session quotas/storage, cookie name, exact configured origins and trusted-proxy boundary, schema, retention/cleanup, and the atomic MFA transition before implementation.

## Research: implemented authentication alternatives

Research-only comparison requested during T167; no product, dependency, license exception, architecture change or implementation is selected.

### Django authentication stack plus django-otp

[Django 5.2](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/releases/5.2/) is an LTS release under the project's [BSD-3-Clause license](https://github.com/django/django/blob/stable/5.2.x/LICENSE). Its maintained framework includes authentication, database-backed sessions, CSRF middleware, SQLite support and database transactions. Django's `login()` rotates the session key and CSRF secret; `logout()` flushes the session. The CSRF middleware checks the token and Origin. [django-otp](https://django-otp-official.readthedocs.io/en/stable/overview.html) supplies an OTP framework and TOTP plugin; its upstream [Unlicense](https://github.com/django-otp/django-otp/blob/master/LICENSE) is a public-domain dedication. This permits a single Python-owned browser-auth boundary and one SQLite transaction domain, avoiding the current Node/Python preauth-consumption handoff if the architecture is consolidated.

This is not a drop-in or already integrated solution. To gain the single-owner benefit, the browser-facing authentication, session, CSRF and lifecycle-audit writes must be owned by the same Django service/database transaction; the current TypeScript access-service boundary and approved trust responsibilities would need redesign. Django's stock DB session backend stores the raw session key as its lookup/key field and generates 32 characters from lowercase ASCII letters and digits (about 165 bits), unlike the selected hash-only 256-bit bearer-token policy; a custom backend would need to change both storage and generation, or the owner would need to review an explicit policy change. The default CSRF design also differs from the accepted ADR 0007 synchronizer-token/preauth contract; `CSRF_USE_SESSIONS` is a possible setting to investigate, not proof of equivalence. Django's standard `login()` cycles the key for an anonymous session but retains its data; a selected design must explicitly flush the preauth session before login to preserve the requirement not to reuse/elevate pre-session state. The stock `SessionMiddleware` saves session changes during response processing, after the view; wrapping the view in `transaction.atomic` alone therefore does not prove that the session row and lifecycle-audit event commit together. A custom store/middleware or another verified transaction boundary is needed. TOTP flow, password throttling, audit policy, session revocation and the exact public schema still require application work and tests.

### Keycloak with OpenID Connect

[Keycloak](https://www.keycloak.org/docs/latest/server_admin/) is a free [Apache-2.0](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/blob/main/LICENSE.txt) identity server with built-in TOTP, recovery codes, browser sessions and OIDC. Its official client guidance recommends the Authorization Code flow for web applications and explicitly warns against the direct username/password grant under current OAuth security best practice. This keeps passwords and TOTP entry at the identity provider and replaces custom application login with a standard redirect-based flow.

Keycloak is a separate server, and its supported production database list excludes SQLite; its built-in development file database is explicitly not suitable for production ([database support](https://www.keycloak.org/server/db)). It therefore requires a supported database and new service/deployment/backup/recovery work. The application still needs a reviewed OIDC relying-party/session and authorization boundary; adopting Keycloak alone does not make the application's own cookie, audit or protected-operation transaction atomic. ARM64 resource and support acceptance would also need target evidence.

### Research conclusion

Django is the closer candidate if retaining Python and SQLite is more important than retaining the Node-owned public auth boundary; Keycloak is the more turnkey identity product if accepting a separate service and supported production database is reasonable. Neither is presently shown to satisfy every selected control without custom work. Recommend evaluating Django first as an architecture alternative, with explicit tests for session-key-at-rest handling and atomic audit/session persistence; do not install it or change the accepted ADRs until the owner selects the direction and G-ARCH/G-CONTRACT/G-SECURITY review it. Sources: [Django auth](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/topics/auth/default/), [Django CSRF](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/ref/csrf/), [Django sessions](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/topics/http/sessions/), [Django transactions](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/topics/db/transactions/), [django-otp overview](https://django-otp-official.readthedocs.io/en/stable/overview.html), [Keycloak OIDC flows](https://www.keycloak.org/securing-apps/oidc-layers), and [Keycloak database support](https://www.keycloak.org/server/db).

## Review and validation plan

Before implementation, obtain G-SECURITY approval for the open decisions and preserve accepted requirements. T026–T035 should include negative tests for session fixation, expiry and revocation; concurrent or replayed reset/MFA tokens; MFA bypass; cross-user and guest capability access; channel elevation; forged/malformed proxy origin; IPv6 and shared-origin limits; guest expiry, restart, queue cancellation and cleanup; CSRF, stale confirmations and concurrent tabs; permission changes during work; and audit unavailability. Test authorization at execution time and ensure confirmation never elevates privileges. Measure password verification latency/contention on the real Pi before final hardware acceptance. No implementation, security gate approval, tests or hardware acceptance are claimed by this proposal.
