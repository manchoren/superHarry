## First-chat scope and advisory freshness recommendation (2026-10-31)

The owner selected **T042, the basic chat slice with simulated responses**, as the initial T014 review milestone on 2026-10-31; real provider routing and local inference belong to the later T057 milestone. **Owner decision (2026-10-31):** the owner selected the recommended maximum; use a limit of 24 hours for an eligible cache. The proposed operational policy is to permit a previously complete and integrity-verified local advisory cache for at most **24 hours** since its last successful complete synchronization with its authenticated source, measured at the time of each approval scan. Attempt a current refresh first. At exactly 24 hours the cache remains eligible; any greater age, future/unknown timestamp, failed integrity or provenance verification, incomplete synchronization, missing ecosystem coverage or partial scan blocks the mandatory advisory check and therefore approval. A successful synchronization means all required pages/deltas were obtained and verified with no unfilled cursor or time gap; an individual advisory's modification date or a newly copied file is not the synchronization timestamp. Recheck freshness at each subsequent approval; an old report does not extend the cache lifetime. Preserve source identity, sync time, coverage, verification results and scan output in redacted evidence. Clock source, authenticated transport/trust, cache storage/integrity mechanism, exact databases and tool-specific synchronization procedures remain to be specified and approved; this recommendation does not authorize installing tools or bypass any check.

**Basis and limits:** [NIST's NVD data-feed documentation](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/data-feeds) says its recent/modified feeds update approximately every two hours, its yearly feeds daily, and the modified feed covers only changes in the previous eight days. It recommends checking feed metadata, which includes a SHA-256 digest; a digest alone does not authenticate the origin. These are NVD-specific update characteristics, not an endorsed cache-age policy for npm, Python or every advisory source. The 24-hour value is a proposed practical upper bound that limits offline staleness to one day, not a NIST recommendation or a guarantee that vulnerabilities published since the last synchronization are detected. Each chosen source must support a verifiable complete and current-enough cache; otherwise block. The owner selected the 24-hour recommendation; source-specific stricter limits and the remaining operational details require review before use.
