# Resource governance and durable action-state proposal

T010 design proposal, 2026-10-30. **Proposal only; no implementation or approval is claimed.** Owner approval is pending for new resource thresholds and unresolved operating policies. G-MODEL and G-CONTRACT review, plus applicable security and operations approval, remain open. Constitution baseline 2026-10-29 SHA-256 `426923873e828b7a0e8fd63b88a5a9b5e01b35f9c2e3bb965c322931d1d9835a`. This proposal is based on the [project plan](../project_plan.md), [contracts proposal](contracts-proposal.md), [model-routing proposal](model-routing-proposal.md), and [threat model](threat-model.md); it does not extend their approvals.

## Scope and fixed boundaries

One trusted coordinator arbitrates all heavy workloads from the web app, linked channels, the persistent scheduler, and administrative workflows. The shared slot covers local model inference, local STT, TTS, OCR, and heavy sandbox executions. No channel, scheduled task, admin request, worker, or model can bypass admission. Lightweight control-plane work (authentication, authorization, health, cancellation, audit, and queue management) must remain responsive under load.

The slot controls active heavy execution, not authorization. Recheck the current owner, role, channel ceiling, operation permission, and any required confirmation at admission and before each consequential side effect. Jobs run with their requesting owner's permissions. Admin status does not transfer ownership or authorize an operation through another user's job. Administrative-only actions require the web app. Channel-linked admin identities remain capped at `user`; guests have no tools, persistent memory, external-model access, sandbox, or messaging channels. Guest requests use the local model only and cannot claim or control the shared display.

These are existing plan constraints, not new approvals: guest requests are low-priority and idle-only, with at most one queued or running question per guest session and a configurable 10-minute queue timeout; sandbox runs have the accepted initial per-run limits of 60 seconds, one CPU-equivalent, 512 MB RAM, 256 MB temporary disk, 64 processes, and 10 MB combined console output. Resource governance does not relax those limits or other role and channel restrictions.

## Admission, fairness, and thresholds

Proposed admission flow:

1. Authenticate and authorize the requester; validate the operation, inputs, owner-scoped references, required confirmations, and mandatory agent context.
2. Classify the work and estimate its resource needs. Unknown or ambiguous work is treated as heavy. If classification, required capacity data, or safe resource headroom is unavailable, do not start it.
3. Persist an owner-bound operation and enqueue it under its approved priority and deadline. Admission is atomic: only the coordinator can assign the slot, and only one live worker generation may hold it.
4. Recheck authorization and resource availability immediately before dispatch. The worker reports start and confirmed stop with its generation ID; a stale completion cannot release a newer worker's slot.
5. Release the slot only after confirmed worker termination and safe settlement of its resource reservation. A disconnected client, cancellation request, timeout, or process-manager restart is not proof that the worker stopped.

Proposed priority policy: eligible authenticated `user` and `admin` work shares the higher start-priority class; role alone does not rank an admin's ordinary job above a user's. Guest work starts only when no eligible higher-priority job is waiting and the Pi is otherwise idle. An already-running guest answer is not preempted solely because higher-priority work arrives. Within a class, use FIFO ordering with per-owner fair rotation so one principal cannot fill all starts; enforce the accepted one-pending-or-running-question guest limit. Cron and channel jobs inherit their owner's class and permissions. Do not promise bounded guest wait under sustained higher-priority load: expire stale guest work under its accepted timeout and report that the guest may retry. The exact fairness algorithm, aging policy, per-owner pending limits, and starvation policy remain proposals.

**Thresholds — owner approval pending:** this proposal sets no new numeric RAM/headroom reserve, CPU or temperature limit, queue capacity, per-owner quota, worker-start deadline, cancellation grace period, execution deadline, retry count, or retry/backoff ceiling. These values require owner approval before adoption, with real-Pi measurements where hardware performance is relevant; any admin configurability must remain inside approved bounds. Existing accepted guest and sandbox values above are not reopened by this proposal. Once required thresholds are approved, record their units, scope, defaults, permitted admin range, measurement source, and fail-closed behavior. Until then, unavailable metrics or unknown headroom block new heavy admission rather than assuming capacity. A loaded but idle model may remain resident only if the approved memory budget allows it. Reserve control-plane capacity so saturation cannot block cancellation, authentication, or mandatory audit.

## Cancellation, deadlines, and retries

Cancellation is an owner-authorized request, not a promise of rollback. A requester may cancel their own queued or awaiting-provider operation; a guest may cancel only their own session's work. Admin-wide operational cancellation, if needed, requires a separately approved capability and audited web-app action; admin status must not silently grant control of another user's content. Cancellation is idempotent and rechecks authorization.

- Before dispatch, cancellation can transition the operation to `cancelled`; release any reservation only when safe.
- During execution, persist `cancelling`, signal the worker, and wait for confirmed process termination. The worker's slot remains occupied until then. A configured escalation deadline and process-supervisor response need owner/operations approval. If termination cannot be established, quarantine the worker, block new heavy work, and surface a recoverable error.
- If an external effect may already have occurred, cancellation cannot undo it by assumption. Reconcile from authoritative evidence; otherwise persist `unknown`, tell the owner, and do not automatically repeat the action.
- If completion wins a cancellation race, report the confirmed completion. A lost client connection does not cancel the operation; reconnects read persisted state and never imply that work was restarted.

Define separate queue, worker-start, provider-connect/response, and execution deadlines rather than one ambiguous timeout. Their values and whether they are configurable are **pending owner approval**, except for the already accepted configurable 10-minute guest queue timeout and accepted sandbox per-run limit. An elapsed queue deadline before dispatch may expire the operation. A timeout after dispatch is not proof of failure, zero provider charge, stopped compute, or absence of a side effect.

Retry only a definitively safe, repeatable operation or a verified idempotent operation, with bounded attempts and backoff approved in advance. Do not retry an uncertain consequential effect, duplicate a charge, or replay a confirmation. Revalidate permission and confirmation before every new dispatch. Provider failures may fall back directly to local execution only when the model-routing proposal's capability, mandatory-context, budget, authorization, and side-effect conditions all hold. Otherwise preserve the work as `awaiting_provider`, notify its owner, and allow cancellation. Keep uncertain provider charges reserved until reconciled, as proposed in T009; no alternate premium provider is implied.

## Protected context and trimmable context

Every runtime-agent invocation, delegated runtime agent, model, and channel must receive the **complete current constitution** as protected instruction context. Never summarize, truncate, omit, or move it into ordinary user content. Verify complete delivery and track the constitution version/hash. Count tokens with the selected model's tokenizer after accounting for the full constitution, mandatory protected instructions, role-scoped tool definitions required for the request, user task, other required inputs, and a reserved output budget. Recount when the model, tokenizer, prompt format, constitution, or tool set changes. Do not expose tools a requester is not authorized to use.

Only optional context may be trimmed to fit: older conversation history not needed for the current task, optional retrieved material, nonessential documents, and other non-mandatory context. Preserve source, owner scope, and untrusted-data labels when retaining or summarizing any such material. Never trim fixed directives, the constitution, authorization scope, required confirmation details, the current user request, or schemas for tools that are actually offered. Guests receive no user/global memory or persistent context.

If tokenization or delivery cannot be verified, or mandatory context plus required request material and output reserve cannot fit after optional context is removed, block the agent/tool action and return an explicit safe error. Do not fall back to a model or channel that cannot carry the full protected context, and do not let the model decide whether the omission is acceptable.

## Durable operation and side-effect lifecycle

Persist each accepted operation before acknowledging it. The record should contain an opaque operation ID, owner, role/channel at intake, authorization revision, validated intent reference, idempotency key scoped to owner and payload, creation/deadline data, state, attempt, worker generation, and an audit-safe outcome. Keep secrets and unnecessary prompt or payload content out of audit metadata; store any required payload separately with owner-scoped access controls. Persist a dispatch-intent record before each external side effect and a definitive outcome afterward. A confirmation is tied to the exact validated intent and owner; changed intent or stale authority requires fresh authorization and, when applicable, fresh confirmation.

Proposed durable states and transitions:

| State | Allowed outcomes |
|---|---|
| `queued` | `running` after fresh admission and authorization; `awaiting_confirmation` when required; `cancelled` before dispatch; `expired` at its queue deadline. |
| `awaiting_confirmation` | `queued` only after valid confirmation of the unchanged intent and a fresh permission check; `cancelled` or `expired` if withdrawn or timed out. |
| `running` | `dispatching` before each side effect; `completed` on confirmed success; `failed` on definitive failure; `cancelling` on request; `awaiting_provider` when safe local continuation is unavailable; `unknown` when an effect or its outcome is uncertain. |
| `dispatching` | A write-ahead marker for an individual side effect. Return to `running` only after recording an authoritative effect outcome when more work remains; otherwise transition to `completed`, `failed`, or `cancelling` as applicable. Move to `unknown` if the outcome cannot be established. A crash or lost response in this state must not trigger automatic replay. |
| `cancelling` | `cancelled` only after worker stop is confirmed and the effect outcome is known; `completed` if completion won the race; otherwise `unknown`. |
| `awaiting_provider` | Return to `queued` only after provider/budget recovery, current authorization, and any required fresh confirmation; or `cancelled` by its owner. No side effect is repeated merely because service returned. |
| `unknown` | Remains blocked from automatic retry. Notify the owner and reconcile using authoritative evidence if available; a new consequential action is a new operation with current authorization and fresh confirmation where required. |
| `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled`, `expired` | Terminal for this operation. Any repeat is a new operation subject to normal checks. |

For crashes, stale workers, failed audit writes, or provider responses lost after dispatch, fail closed. State changes and mandatory security audit should share a transaction or use a reviewed durable write-ahead protocol; do not dispatch a privileged side effect if its mandatory audit intent cannot be durably recorded. On restart, reconcile worker processes before slot reuse. Resume only from a persisted safe checkpoint or verified idempotency/reconciliation result; otherwise preserve `unknown` and require owner-visible resolution. Idempotency-key reuse with a different validated payload is rejected. Exact retention, storage schema, transaction mechanism, cross-restart event replay, and resolution workflow remain open G-CONTRACT/G-OPS details; this proposal does not claim an approved API or schema.

## Verification obligations and open decisions

Before implementation, obtain owner approval for the new threshold and timeout/retry values; resolve G-MODEL, G-CONTRACT, and applicable security/operations questions; and measure hardware-dependent limits on the real Pi. Emulation can validate behavior but cannot establish Pi performance, thermal behavior, or physical isolation.

Mocked tests should cover concurrent admissions across all heavy classes and entry points; guest idle priority, one-pending limit and expiry; fair ordering under competing owners; queue starvation; unavailable or stale metrics; context overflow, tokenizer failure and incomplete protected delivery; cancellation with delayed or unconfirmed worker exit; stale worker-generation completion; restart with an orphan worker; provider timeout, unknown charge and safe local fallback; stale permission and invalidated confirmation; duplicate idempotency keys; audit persistence failure; and crashes before, during, and after side-effect dispatch. Verify that unknown outcomes are never retried, reservations are not released prematurely, and authentication, health, cancellation, and audit remain responsive under synthetic saturation. No implementation, tests, measurements, or approvals are claimed by this design.
