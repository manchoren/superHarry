# Free-provider candidates

Owner direction, 2026-11-04: configure an administrator-controlled provider order, block routes whose zero total cost cannot be verified, and use local inference if all free routes are unavailable and local inference is available; otherwise inform the user. A provider that uses conversations to improve models requires disclosure and explicit administrator enablement. Guests remain local-only. OpenAI is disabled for now.

Provider terms, prices, models and account limits can change. Credentials stay outside the repository, and credential presence alone never admits a provider. Development admission is not release acceptance: T178/T179 remain open until their remaining integration, recovery, quota, target-VM and release criteria pass.

## Current development status — 2026-11-05

### Groq: conditionally admitted

The owner confirmed that the configured Groq account is on the Free plan and has no card or other billing mechanism, and authorized Groq only while those conditions remain true. The selected development model is `openai/gpt-oss-20b`. Local non-resetting allowances are 100 generation requests and 100,000 tokens; exhaustion requires manual reconciliation and does not reset automatically. If the plan, payment configuration or zero-cost evidence changes or cannot be verified, disable Groq and fail closed.

The development integration sends the complete constitution, bounded conversation context and current request through the fixed-origin chat-completions transport. GPT-OSS reasoning is requested with `include_reasoning: false`, so reasoning content is not returned to the application. Official references: [OpenAI-compatible API](https://console.groq.com/docs/openai), [GPT-OSS reasoning controls](https://console.groq.com/docs/reasoning), [rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits), [billing](https://console.groq.com/docs/billing-faqs), and [data handling](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data).

One bounded live synthetic request succeeded after the reasoning-output control was added. It delivered the complete protected constitution and settled 1 request and 1,760 total tokens (1,739 input and 21 output). No credential, prompt or response content was recorded. An earlier request reached Groq but returned a response that the then-current strict validator could not use; its outcome remains unknown and holds reservations for 1 request and 9,812 tokens. It was not retried automatically or released without correlated evidence.

Groq does not currently document a pre-dispatch token-count endpoint or its exact server chat template. The UTF-8 byte admission counter is therefore a provisional surrogate with additional headroom, not a proven upper bound, Groq tokenization or an exact input count. Successful responses are reconciled from provider-reported usage. Exact tokenizer/context conformance, account-wide usage reconciliation, remote cancellation and target-VM acceptance remain open. Groq states that inference inputs and outputs are not retained by default, but may be retained for reliability or abuse investigations for up to 30 days unless the administrator enables the applicable [data controls](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data). The account's actual settings remain to be verified.

### OpenRouter: disabled

OpenRouter remains disabled. A `:free` model suffix and zero prompt/completion price caps do not prove zero total cost because account state, auxiliary services and route pricing can still matter. The reviewed Liquid free route may retain prompts and outputs for model improvement, while the current request policy requires `data_collection: deny` and zero-data-retention routing. No OpenRouter route is admitted until an exact zero-cost endpoint satisfies the selected privacy policy, or the owner explicitly chooses and enables a different disclosed data-treatment policy.

OpenRouter's [retention documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr) says request-level `zdr: true` follows account or guardrail settings that can permit safety-retention endpoints; choosing strict ZDR requires checking those settings as well. The same documentation describes an exception for material flagged during safety screening. The application caps prompt, completion, per-request and image prices at zero, but account, BYOK and auxiliary charges still require separate verification. No change to a privacy setting or provider admission is implied by these findings.

On 2026-11-05 the owner confirmed that the exact `liquid/lfm-2.5-2.6b:free` / `liquid/fp8` route has zero total cost with no billable BYOK, and that strict ZDR without safety-retention endpoints is configured for the route. The owner selected strict ZDR and approved non-resetting development limits of 100 requests and 100,000 tokens. These are owner attestations, not independent account inspection or release acceptance. They do not authorize any other route, a paid fallback, production deployment or automatic retry; disable the route if the account, cost, privacy or model conditions cease to hold or cannot be verified.

The application restricts route identity and rejects shared limits above the approved ceilings. The owner accepts an approximate UTF-8 byte estimate and possible quota overrun; `SUPERHARRY_OPENROUTER_APPROXIMATE_QUOTA=1` opts in separately and defaults off. The estimate is not a demonstrated token bound. The ledger can record valid reported overage without discarding other holds; unknown results remain held. [OpenRouter usage accounting](https://openrouter.ai/docs/cookbook/administration/usage-accounting) reports native-tokenizer counts after a response, not a pre-dispatch bound.

Selection and dispatch nevertheless remain blocked to protect the constitution. The request explicitly disables context compression. OpenRouter's [message-transform documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/message-transforms) says that when compression is disabled an over-context request fails, but its [plugin documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/plugins) also says an account or organization can enable "Prevent overrides", preventing a request from disabling an enforced default plugin. Other default plugins can mutate requests too. The effective account/organization settings and any upstream `liquid/fp8` truncation behavior have not been verified. Local serialization and synthetic tests cannot establish them, nor is an effective-plugin response attestation established here. Reliable evidence and a fail-closed control for those conditions are still needed. No OpenRouter quotas were provisioned by this follow-up.

Official references: [free-model collection](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models), [provider routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection), [privacy routing controls](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/get-started/sovereign-ai), and [pricing](https://openrouter.ai/pricing/). OpenRouter HTTP 429 remains an unknown outcome because the reviewed contract does not prove that every platform or upstream 429 precedes generation; it must not trigger automatic failover.

## Shared controls and remaining limits

Provider order stays administrator-configurable. Every attempted route must independently satisfy admission, authenticated-user role, protected-context, privacy, zero-cost, quota and safe-retry checks. Unknown or partial outcomes retain their quota reservations and are never blindly repeated elsewhere. Local inference fallback is not yet available. Explicit provider/model selection, exact context conformance, durable provider reconciliation and target-VM validation remain part of T178/T179 rather than being established by this document.

Development credentials may be supplied locally through the ignored `.env` as `GROQ_API_KEY`, `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` or `GEMINI_API_KEY`; deployments use environment variables or an approved secrets facility. Never paste credential values into the repository, documentation, chat or validation output.
