Status: accepted in part by the project owner on 2026-10-30; see [ADR 0004](adr/0004-security-decisions.md). Not implemented. Tasks T007–T008 and T026–T035; gates G-SECURITY and, for OS identities, proxy and secret delivery, G-OPS. Constitution baseline 2026-10-29, SHA-256 `426923873e828b7a0e8fd63b88a5a9b5e01b35f9c2e3bb965c322931d1d9835a`. This supplements the [authentication proposal](auth-policy-proposal.md), [bootstrap proposal](bootstrap-recovery-proposal.md), and [approved MFA recovery outcome](adr/0003-lost-mfa-recovery.md). Accepted guest limits, role boundaries and bootstrap token requirements remain binding. Concrete candidates below are approved within the limited scope recorded in ADR 0004; explicitly open details remain pending. Exact libraries, schemas, interfaces and OS mechanisms require separate approval.

## Decision package

| Topic | Candidate and tradeoff |
|---|---|
| Passwords | Require at least 15 Unicode code points for every account, including with optional MFA; allow at least 64, accept Unicode and spaces, and normalize to NFC consistently before checking and hashing. Avoid composition rules and periodic rotation; reject known compromised or common whole-password values. Set an upper input limit after denial-of-service testing; never truncate. A shorter minimum with MFA is more convenient but complicates disabling or recovering MFA. A maintained local blocklist avoids sending candidate passwords externally; its source, license, updates and integrity remain open. Fail closed for new or changed passwords if the required list is unavailable, while allowing existing login. [NIST SP 800-63B](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html) supports these password practices; this is guidance, not a claim of federal assurance compliance. |
| Argon2id | Start with memory 19 MiB, iterations 2 and parallelism 1, with a unique random salt per password and encoded parameters. Measure target-device latency and contention before hardware acceptance; bound simultaneous verification and queue work. Rehash on successful login when approved parameters change. A stronger configuration may be preferable if measurements allow. [OWASP Password Storage](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html) lists this and other minimum cost tradeoffs. |
| Authentication throttling | Propose account and verified-origin counters, generic denial and progressive temporary delay, never a permanent attacker-triggered lockout. Candidate: after five failures for an account in 15 minutes, delay further attempts for 15 minutes, with additional per-origin limiting. Persist counters across restart, bound storage and audit denials without credentials. Global/origin rates, retention and successful-login reset remain decisions; NAT clients can share an origin. Avoid revealing account existence via responses or materially different processing. |
| Sessions | Propose a 256-bit random opaque identifier with a server-side verifier; host-only `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict` cookie, path `/`, no domain attribute, over HTTPS. Enforce server-side 30-minute idle and 12-hour absolute account-session limits; rotate at login and privilege changes and revoke at logout, reset, disablement and role changes. Background polling or SSE alone must not extend inactivity. Shorter limits reduce exposure but interrupt work; guest sessions retain their separately accepted 24-hour expiry. [OWASP Session Management](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html) recommends server-side idle and absolute expiration tailored to risk. |
| TOTP | Candidate: 30-second steps, six digits, unique cryptographically random secret per account; accept current and one prior/next step, rate-limit attempts and atomically reject already accepted steps. Enroll after recent reauthentication and valid-code confirmation. Never log, expose the seed to the agent or include it in downloadable backups. Narrower windows reduce exposure but tolerate less clock drift. [RFC 6238](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6238) defines TOTP and requires rejection of an already accepted OTP. Encryption, key custody and clock/race handling remain open; TOTP is not phishing-resistant. |
| Sensitive actions | Propose reauthentication within five minutes using password and configured TOTP; use a random single-use confirmation bound to actor, session, action, target, exact parameters and current state, expiring after five minutes and invalidated by session revocation or state change. Stronger plan requirements prevail. Per-action reauthentication offers tighter assurance at a usability cost. The operation list and treatment of actions exceeding five minutes need approval. Recheck authorization at execution; a channel cannot grant administrative authority. |
| Password reset | Propose a 256-bit random single-use token with stored verifier and ten-minute expiration. Authenticate the approved recovery path and bind the token to the account; revoke sessions and outstanding tokens atomically after successful reset. Submit tokens in a protected body, never in a URL or log. An admin-assisted web flow versus authorized local OS-admin recovery requires selection; no mail or other channel is implied. Password reset must not silently remove or bypass an enrolled factor. |
| Lost MFA recovery | The owner approved OS-admin recovery with explicit confirmation, audit and mandatory fresh MFA enrollment. Candidate: an elevated local operation revokes existing sessions and factor, sets a durable re-enrollment-required state, and permits only password-verified, short-lived enrollment in the trusted web interface until a new factor is verified. No normal app actions or channel use during that state. OS-admin proof, privilege, enrollment deadline, restart behavior, bound enrollment credential and transactional audit remain open. OS-admin compromise can initiate recovery; fail closed if identity verification or durable audit is unavailable. |
| Proxy and origin | Accept forwarding information only from explicitly configured trusted immediate peers; the edge strips inbound client forwarding headers and creates its own. Derive normalized IPv4/IPv6 origin from a validated chain, rejecting ambiguous input. Direct loopback setup and LAN listeners remain separate. Trusted proxy identity, hop count, headers, Tailscale path, IPv6 handling and NAT policy require G-SECURITY/G-OPS and deployment tests. IP origin is a rate-limit signal, not person identity. No public exposure or port forwarding. |
| Audit and failure | Keep durable records of authentication and privileged attempts and outcomes without secrets or message content. Privileged changes fail closed when required audit persistence is unavailable; record intent before external side effects and reconcile uncertain outcomes instead of blindly retrying. Atomicity, transaction boundaries, retention, protection and partial-failure recovery require approved storage and operations design. Audit availability is independent of optional diagnostics. |

## Required review and validation

The owner may accept or change individual rows; approval of one does not approve all. Record exact scope, reviewer, date and evidence in an ADR. Select and review exact implementations and dependencies separately; agree schema and contracts before changing them. Resolve breach-list provenance and license, encryption key custody and recovery, verifier algorithm, CSRF design, session and clock behavior, and MFA re-enrollment state before implementation. Verify no accepted plan requirement is weakened.

Synthetic negative tests must cover expired or reused tokens, concurrent redemption, OTP replay and clock changes, session fixation and revocation, interrupted enrollment and audit failure, forged forwarding headers, cross-owner access, guest restrictions, rate-limiter restart and shared-origin starvation. Measure password-verification latency and contention on the real Pi before final hardware acceptance. No integration, live credentials, implementation, installation or tests are claimed by this proposal.
